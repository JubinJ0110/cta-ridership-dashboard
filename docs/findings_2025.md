# 2025 in focus: the recovery is real, and it is uneven

2025 closed at **338.0 million rides**, up from 2019's 455.7 million — still
25.8% below the pre-pandemic baseline, but the direction is consistent:
every year since 2020 has been higher than the last, and 2026 is running
+2.6% ahead of 2025 through August.

## What is driving the recovery

- **Weekends first.** In 2024, Saturday ridership was back to 79.9% of 2019
  and Sunday/holiday to 80.2%, while weekdays sat at 64.9%. The commuter is
  the missing rider: leisure and errand trips came back, the 9-to-5 trip did
  not — at least not at the same rate.
- **Bus over rail.** Bus share rose from 52.1% of rides in 2019 to 59.1% in
  2025. Rail depends more on downtown office commuting; bus serves more
  local, all-day trips, which recovered faster.
- **The floor held.** Monthly rides did not pass 30 million again until
  October 2024 — four and a half years after the April 2020 trough of
  8.1 million. The recovery was slow, then steady, not a snap-back.

## What to watch next

1. Whether weekday ridership keeps closing the gap as return-to-office
   policies settle — that is the single biggest lever left.
2. Whether bus share stays elevated or rail reclaims share as downtown
   commuting returns.
3. Whether 2026 finishes above 2025 by more than the current +2.6% pace;
   the second half of the year is seasonally the strongest.

## Honest limits

These are descriptive patterns from one dataset. They do not prove that
remote work caused the weekday lag, or that any single policy moved the
numbers — they show where the rides are and are not. See
`docs/methodology.md` for the caveats.
